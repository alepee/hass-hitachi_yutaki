One winter day

What your heat pump does
while you are not looking.

A heat pump works all day, mostly out of sight. Follow one cold day, from the first frost to the evening check, and see what the integration shows you along the way.

  1. 05:40Cold start

    −4 °C outside. The house is already warm.

    Outdoor temperature compensation raises the water temperature as the night gets colder. You see every circuit, the setpoints and the flow temperatures, and you can adjust heating, cooling and ECO mode from Home Assistant.

    One or two circuits, each with its own climate entity. The integration detects your model and only creates what your installation actually has.

    Circuit 1
    🔥ModeHeating
    🌡️Room20.4 °C → 20.5 °C
    💧Water outlet38 °C
    ❄️Outdoor−4 °C
  2. 07:12Defrost

    Ice on the coil. Nothing to count.

    In the cold and damp, the outdoor coil frosts over and the unit briefly runs in reverse to clear it. The integration knows: defrost cycles and the inertia right after the compressor stops are left out of your thermal energy, so your numbers stay honest.

    Thermal power heating · this morning
    Counted as heatingDefrost, left out
  3. 12:30Hot water

    Hot water first, then back to the radiators.

    When the unit switches to domestic hot water, the circuits pause. Home Assistant shows what is really happening, not just what was asked: the circuit reads idle while the tank heats up.

    Boost the tank before guests arrive, or run an anti-legionella cycle on demand. Swimming pools are supported too.

    Domestic hot water
    🚿Tank49 °C → 55 °C
    ⚡BoostOn
    Circuit 1
    ⏸ActivityIdle · hot water in progress
  4. 18:30Efficiency

    So, was it worth it?

    The coefficient of performance tells you how much heat you got for each kilowatt-hour of electricity. It is measured separately for heating, cooling, hot water and pool, and each value says how much you can trust it.

    Add your own water temperature sensors or energy meter, and the integration uses them for more precise figures.

  5. Weeks laterEarly warning · beta

    Small drifts, caught early.

    Two optional detectors watch for problems that build up quietly: a compressor that starts and stops too often, and a slow loss of refrigerant. Both run locally, learn your installation, and raise a repair issue in Home Assistant only when the signal holds over several days.

    Off by default. They give you a reason to call your installer early, they do not replace the regular inspection.

Built to stay home

Your heat pump.
Your data.

Local first

The integration talks to the Modbus gateway on your own network. No cloud account, no subscription, and it keeps working when the internet does not.

Opt-in, always

Anonymous telemetry is off until you turn it on, and so are the beta detectors. A tiny daily ping counts installations by model and version, with no identifier, and you can switch it off in the options.

Open and shared

MIT licensed, translated by volunteers on Weblate, and shaped by owners who report what their units actually do. Every finding goes back into the code for everyone.

Supported hardware

What you need.

A Hitachi air-to-water heat pump, a Modbus gateway connected to it, and Home Assistant on the same network. Not sure which generation you have? Try the model decoder.

Heat pumps
  • Yutaki S 2016 and newer · earlier units in beta
  • Yutaki S Combi 2016 and newer · earlier units in beta
  • Yutaki S80 dual compressor
  • Yutaki M monobloc
  • Yutampo R32 hot water only
Modbus gateways
  • ATW-MBS-02 Hitachi, single unit
  • HC-A16MB / HC-A64MB Hitachi, one or several units

For gateway makers

The missing piece
is the gateway.

Many owners who want to connect their heat pump stop at the same step: Hitachi's own gateways are expensive and often out of stock. The integration is built so that each gateway is a separate adapter. If your product talks to Hitachi heat pumps, supporting it is a clean addition, and it reaches owners who are actively looking for one.

Get started

Bring it home.

Install through HACS, the Home Assistant Community Store, as a custom repository.

https://github.com/alepee/hass-hitachi_yutaki
Or open it in HACS directly ↗
01 · Connect

Wire a supported Modbus gateway to your heat pump and plug it into your network.

02 · Install

Add the repository above in HACS, download the integration and restart Home Assistant.

03 · Add

In Settings › Devices & Services, add Hitachi Heat Pump. Your model is detected for you.

One setting to check on the heat pump.

The external control mode must not be set to Local. Air, Water or Total all work, and Air suits most installations. Read the configuration guide ↗
